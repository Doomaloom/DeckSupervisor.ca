# DeckSupervisor Citywide IT Demo Speaker Notes

Audience: citywide IT implementation review  
Target length: 15-20 minutes  
Tone: neutral product voice, operational value first

## Slide 1: Decksupervisor.ca

Introduce DeckSupervisor as a practical aquatics operations tool for scaling repeatable session prep across facilities. Keep the framing operational: faster prep, cleaner handoffs, and supervisor visibility.

## Slide 2: The Problem with Current Systems

The reason for the app is staff time. Prep work is spread across exports, spreadsheets, print screens, and manual checking. Supervisors also need visibility without asking every staff member to send files.

## Slide 3: Understanding User Needs

Explain that the workflow comes from deck supervisor, programmer, and day-to-day preparation needs. The point is not a one-off tool; it is a repeatable process that can work across facilities.

## Slide 4: What Decksupervisor.ca Does

DeckSupervisor turns roster exports into a repeatable workflow. It supports schematics, rosters, print materials, notes, sharing, and planning from one place.

## Slide 5: Streamlined Workflows

Use this as the map for the demo: part-time workflow, full-time workflow, and session planner. Mention that security and privacy are built into how these workflows are shared.

## Slide 6: Part-Time Session Prep

Part-time users handle session-level prep. The workflow starts from a roster export and carries through setup, rosters, printing, and coverage sharing. Guest mode can support local-only work, but the main story here is the saved part-time workflow.

## Slide 7: Upload Roster Export

Do not present upload itself as replacing a major old workflow. The upload is the starting point. The value comes from what the upload enables afterward: session setup, rosters, schematic, and print materials using the same source data.

## Slide 8: Confirm Detected Session

The app detects session details from the CSV. Point out that facility, date range, times, and roster structure come from the export instead of being recreated manually before work can start.

## Slide 9: Dynamic Schematic Tool

Old workflow: manually move cells around in an Excel sheet and print the schematic from Excel. New workflow: organize imported classes visually in the app and use the schematic as a deck-ready reference.

## Slide 10: Comprehensive Roster Management

Old workflow: check against the CSV masterlist and manually set class levels each time materials were printed. New workflow: rosters are grouped by class and level decisions stay in the session workflow.

## Slide 11: Custom Rosters

Custom rosters cover cases where the standard class grouping is not enough, such as adult lessons, combined groups, or instructor-specific organization.

## Slide 12: Effortless Printing Solutions

Old workflow: schematic from Excel, masterlist from cleaned-up CSV, and individual class sheets printed one by one from Xplor. New workflow: one print center prepares the session materials from the imported roster context.

## Slide 13: Share Session Coverage

Old workflow: file handoff and manual explanation. New workflow: share the session through the app so coverage staff can access the right context. Access can be limited to what the coverage person needs.

## Slide 14: Report Cards

Keep this short: report-card tracking is another way session work becomes visible and organized instead of being checked separately.

## Slide 15: Notes

Notes help keep coaching, feedback, and session observations tied to the operating workflow.

## Slide 16: Difference Between Part-Time and Guest

Use this as the first privacy bridge. Guest mode keeps work local. Part-time accounts can save operational context. Uploaded CSV customer details are kept out of shared backend records.

## Slide 17: See Decksupervisor.ca in Action

Live-demo transition for the part-time workflow.

## Slide 18: Full-Time Workflows

For citywide IT, the full-time story is supervisor oversight. Full-time users work across teams, sessions, locations, and terms without collecting separate files from each staff member.

## Slide 19: Supervisor Oversight

Emphasize visibility across staff and sessions. Team and term context replaces one-off file collection.

## Slide 20: Teams And Facilities

Teams and facilities give the app an operating structure. Full-time users can organize staff and locations so workflows can be standardized beyond one pool.

## Slide 21: Requests / Roster Oversight

Show this as the supervisor workflow for reviewing requests and roster-related work in one place. The point is fewer disconnected spreadsheets and messages.

## Slide 22: Team Schematics And Report Cards

Full-time users can review team schematics, track report-card progress, and keep decisions connected to the session workflow.

## Slide 23: Full-Timer Tools

Full-time tools support larger prep tasks that repeat across staff and facilities. Emphasize higher-volume preparation and consistency.

## Slide 24: See Decksupervisor.ca in Action

Live-demo transition for the full-time workflow.

## Slide 25: Session Planner

Introduce the planner as pre-session decision support. It helps organize low-enrollment cancellations, higher-demand replacements, calls, emails, and planned changes.

## Slide 26: Why Session Planning Matters

Before a session starts, low-enrollment classes may need to be cancelled and replaced by higher-demand classes to keep pool time profitable.

## Slide 27: Find Pressure Points

Old workflow: inspect spreadsheets and rosters manually to find classes needing attention. New workflow: the board shows counts, waitlist pressure, and status in one planning view.

## Slide 28: Plan Moves And Alternatives

The planner helps mark planned cancellations or moves and find alternatives for affected customers. Decisions stay connected to the class.

## Slide 29: Calls, Emails, And Scripts

Call scripts are ready, email follow-up is easier, and call status or notes stay with the planned change.

## Slide 30: Planned Changes And Sharing

Planned changes stay organized so staff can review the current plan and avoid duplicate follow-up.

## Slide 31: Security And Privacy

Use plain but specific language. Sensitive customer details from uploaded CSVs are used locally for workflow needs and are not stored in backend records. Shared records store operational context like sessions, schematics, notes, and permissions. Role-based access separates part-time and full-time workflows, and coverage sharing is explicit and limited.

## Slide 32: Key Benefits

Summarize the operational value: less repeated prep, cleaner coverage handoffs, supervisor visibility, consistent workflows across facilities, and privacy-conscious roster handling.

## Slide 33: Cost Benefits

Leave the existing numbers unchanged. Present them as a time-savings estimate already included in the original deck.

## Slide 34: Questions?

Open discussion.

## Slide 35: Thank You for Your Time

Close with the practical implementation message: the app is intended to reduce repeated prep and make the workflow easier to scale across facilities.
